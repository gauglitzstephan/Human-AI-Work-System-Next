# State and intent semantic contract

## Separate responsibilities

`formation.md` supports continued reasoning. `intent.md` supports review, ownership and later work. A conversation transcript is neither. In simple work the chat can carry transient formation; for handoff or interruption save the compact state, and actually read it on return.

### Formation state: minimum useful content

Use readable headings or bullets, not a mandatory database:

- Identity and current intent revision/path; current disposition and why.
- Current problem/outcome understanding, source of material claims and live rival explanation if any.
- Known evidence and accessible source locations; essential limitations/freshness.
- Material assumptions, unknown facts, undecided human choices; which block current framing and why. For a material later question, preserve why it can wait and what finding would reopen the intent.
- Last important correction and affected claims, including any now-superseded conclusion.
- Next useful inquiry and the expected difference its answer would make; attention/revisit condition when relevant.

Keep stable settled content in intent; reference it rather than copy it all. Remove stale pending questions after answers. Keep a short tombstone only for a correction likely to recur. Never compress away the reason an unresolved issue is material. On handoff distinguish source limitations from absence of evidence. Incoming agents must not infer acceptance from an existing file.

### Select before composing

Work from the latest supported understanding, not the accumulated text of the conversation or the previous document. Before choosing headings, identify what the person wants to become different or learn, why it matters, and how the intended result serves that purpose. If that connection is your interpretation, make it reviewable; do not settle it through polished first-person prose.

First sketch the core in working notes: the desired change/learning and its use, the situation that explains the need, and the boundary or unresolved distinction that could change it. Draft from that core, not by shortening every paragraph of the source. Then bring back only missing meaning. Do not print this working outline as another artifact.

For each candidate detail, ask: what specific misunderstanding would its absence cause in this intent? Keep it if the reader would otherwise get the purpose, result, beneficiary, binding limit or consequential uncertainty wrong. Being relevant to the topic or useful during later analysis is not enough. Preserve the actual causal or decision connection, not merely a list of topic nouns. Keep enough of an observed episode to locate the problem without generalizing beyond the evidence.

Place the remaining useful material by function:

| Material | Home |
|---|---|
| The desired change or learning, its use, and what would count as useful | Intent; connect result and success rather than restating them in separate sections. |
| A material framing blocker or unproven premise that changes the purpose/boundary | Intent, with its consequence; fuller evidence and rival interpretations in state. |
| A later unknown that a reader must know to avoid a false commitment | Intent, briefly explaining what remains open; detailed question inventory in state. |
| A reference's consequence for this work | Intent only if it changes the meaning, limits or criteria; attribute locally when consequential. |
| Framework explanations, search history, source assessments, discarded options and revision reasons | Formation state or linked research, not an intent section. |
| Detailed requirements, solution choices or task sequences | Later work; retain an already binding human choice without elaborating a design. |

This is content selection, not evidence deletion. Preserve sources needed for continued reasoning in state. Do not turn every explored consideration into a durable requirement. Do not present your interpretation as the owner's decision; when material, attribute it where it appears. Moving provenance out must not make an uncertain claim sound settled.

For example, a discussion may cover forecasting methods, historical data limits and four possible ways to compare suppliers. If the purpose is to decide whether to replace an unreliable supplier, the intent needs the reliability problem, the intended decision and the material cost/service trade-off. The method discussion stays in state unless the person made one method a binding condition. Explain ordinary terms only when competing meanings would change the intended outcome; do not turn a clear everyday goal into a set of operational rules.

### Compose a reading surface for this work

Start with a meaningful title and the human purpose. State readiness with its concrete reason in ordinary language; put owner, revision and “Akzeptanz dieser Fassung” in a brief secondary line or footer. Acceptance is not recorded unless explicit.

Then give the reader the relevant situation, intended result/use and material boundaries. Use headings that describe those contents. Split only where the reader needs a separate correction target or a distinct relationship; a complex inquiry can still yield a simple intent. Choose [intent-short.md](../assets/intent-short.md) for a clear small case. Use [intent-expanded.md](../assets/intent-expanded.md) when separate context, result and criteria are useful, not because more research occurred. These are examples of composition, not forms to fill.

Keep each claim in one natural home. A result can already explain how success would be recognized. A local attribution can already ground a claim. Neither needs a second section solely to satisfy semantic coverage. If a section answers several different reader questions, separate its actual points or remove supporting commentary; do not hide a source summary inside a broad heading. Use ordinary verbs and the person's vocabulary; explain a scope-controlling term only as far as needed, or mark its specific unresolved meaning. Avoid compound abstractions that hide who wants what to change.

Keep editorial narration out: how the conversation evolved, why the former draft was withdrawn, what this rewrite corrected and which documents were not reread belong in state. State the current uncertainty and source limitation itself in intent only when material to interpreting a claim. Record acceptance once. Do not repeatedly announce that the text is provisional or that no execution was authorized. A draft/status line does not replace local attribution of a genuinely disputed AI interpretation.

Expose a blocking question with the distinction its answer would settle. Distinguish it from unknowns that are the subject of the intended investigation. If no material question remains, explain readiness briefly without inventing an open-questions section. Add economics, guardrails, affected-party conflict or validity only when they alter this intent. Remove all instructional placeholders.

## Claim authority and change

Use local labels where material: “Owner berichtet”, “Quelle”, “AI-Deutung”, “Annahme”, “Unbekannt”, “Entscheidung”. A human statement is evidence of what that person said, not automatically an external fact. A source-backed factual statement is not a human preference. “Unknown” is missing knowledge; “undecided” is an unresolved choice. A constraint requires a source/authority when disputed or consequential.

Record explicit acceptance as who, which revision and the actual acceptance statement or precise reference. Do not ask for redundant confirmation when the user has unambiguously accepted that revision. A material change creates a new revision and invalidates current acceptance; preserve the earlier acceptance as historical. Pure spelling/format changes do not alter meaning but should retain traceable revision handling.

## Reader tasks before saving

Read the actual draft without relying on the conversation. Can you explain its purpose, intended result and their connection from its opening and result passage? Can you locate what is settled, what is proposed and the exact unresolved distinction? Can the owner correct one substantive claim without rewriting an overloaded paragraph? Does each section add information rather than repeat a claim in different words? Repair the text if the reader must reconstruct the main point from scattered sections.

Compare the resulting draft with the source understanding: did selection lose a requested benefit, a binding limit, a material uncertainty, a valid insight or the basis of a consequential claim? Restore needed meaning in its natural location, not by appending a completeness checklist. Readability and semantic fidelity must both hold. These checks assess the document; they are not sections to print in it.

Check that both files agree after material edits and that the actual saved version matches the stated revision. No helper can prove semantic qualification or human acceptance. Do not treat word count, headings or a schema pass as quality evidence.
