# Adaptive inquiry

## Choose a material intervention

Maintain a working explanation, not an interrogation list. Connect the reported observation to a problem hypothesis and a desired change; do not treat these as interchangeable. Ask what plausible alternative would change the intended work. Look for symptom/cause confusion, a desired feature masquerading as a need, an organizational boundary hiding another cause, or a supposed constraint with no owner/source. Use relevant domain practice when it adds understanding. For example, capture problems can arise in capture, clarification, review or trust; reducing handling time can fail if rework or handoffs dominate total delay. These are hypotheses, not diagnoses. State what the available evidence supports and what it does not establish.

Ground an inquiry in available examples and evidence. A good next question distinguishes competing explanations or exposes a necessary value choice. Search/read what can be independently learned. Ask the user about an actual episode or contrast only when that information is unavailable and material. Do not demand personal stories when the supplied evidence is sufficient.

Professional references should change a claim or question. A reference class is a relevant family of comparable problems or practices, not an obligatory literature review or product shortlist. Apply its mechanism: what else could produce this observation, what known limitation challenges the proposed outcome, or what simpler existing practice might already address the need? Distinguish a familiar conceptual lens used as AI analysis from an externally checked claim. Use current primary sources for consequential, uncertain or unstable facts; retain a short citation and its implication. Distinguish law/standard text, authoritative guidance, provider recommendation, observed practice and your synthesis. A legal source does not prove a particular organization's compliance; a vendor claim does not prove user demand. If a source is blocked, preserve the access limit.

## Preference and judgment

Separate unknown facts from undecided values. “We do not know the delay” invites evidence; “we have not chosen convenience versus confidentiality” needs judgment. For an unsettled preference, give two or three fair concrete scenarios with their costs and what remains the same. Allow the owner to reject the framing. Avoid loaded labels such as “safe/professional option” versus “cheap/risky option.” Record only the choice actually made; an illustration is not acceptance and a test scenario is not a personal fact.

You may recommend a framing with reasons, including a contrary view. Keep the recommendation attributable to AI until the human adopts it. Do not silently optimize a measurable proxy over the person’s purpose. No evidence licenses overriding a human value; evidence can correct a factual premise and reopen the choice.

## Whole problem, risk and affected parties

Inspect adjacent causes, dependencies and effects only far enough to determine the right boundary. Distinguish decision owner, direct users, legitimate stakeholders and materially affected nonparticipants. Ask who bears an error or burden. A local convenience improvement may shift work, exclude people or reduce safety elsewhere; reflect this if consequential.

Scale depth to uncertainty, stakes, reversibility and external impact. Low-stakes personal work usually needs no formal stakeholder/risk section. Consequential work may require authoritative source review, conflicting perspectives and unresolved authority clearly visible before qualification. Do not claim assurance from a complete template. An unavailable stakeholder or expert can be a named material uncertainty; you need not contact anyone without authorization.

## Evidence, time and stop

Keep assumptions falsifiable in plain language: what would change the framing if untrue? Distinguish user-reported experience, observed artifact and external evidence. Use dates or source versions when freshness matters. State material validity/reopen triggers (a contract expires, policy changes, existing service becomes adequate), without automatically scheduling a task.

Before another loop, identify which current framing decision its result could change and whether that matters enough to justify effort. A concrete story can settle the meaning of a personal request without proving a general cause or a solution's effectiveness. Do not demand evidence of effectiveness that only later work can produce; do not declare independently answerable domain questions settled merely because personal experience is present. Use the allocation in [boundary.md](boundary.md). Stop with a qualified purpose, a specific investigation/judgment blocker, or a legitimate defer/drop. Reopen if new evidence changes that frame. After expensive research, reassess forward value using remaining costs and evidence; sunk effort is not benefit.

Outcome validation belongs here only as meaning: what observable change would count as useful, for whom, and what harm would negate it? Avoid designing telemetry, test suites, acceptance criteria for features or implementation experiments.
