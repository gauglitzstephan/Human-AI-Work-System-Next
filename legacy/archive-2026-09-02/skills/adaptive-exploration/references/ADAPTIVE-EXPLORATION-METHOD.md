# Adaptive Exploration Method v0.1

**Status:** cross-domain exploration discipline; not a universal design, innovation, strategy, or creativity methodology.

## Purpose

Explore a materially open problem frame, solution class, route, concept, opportunity, or creative direction far enough to avoid premature route collapse and support the next legitimate transition.

## 1. Bind the exploration need

Establish only as far as needed:

- purpose or Work Mission;
- what is materially open: frame, mechanism, route, concept, direction, or hypothesis;
- relevant search-space boundary;
- available evidence and reference basis;
- expected output;
- consequence of premature collapse; and
- stop or transition condition.

Do not require the whole work episode to be fully formed. If one missing basis blocks responsible exploration itself, route that basis to `work-formation`.

## 2. Choose the shallowest adequate exploration regime

Possible regimes include:

- **framing and reframing** — develop materially different interpretations of the need or system;
- **opportunity exploration** — identify distinct ways value, learning, risk reduction, or leverage could arise;
- **route exploration** — identify different causal or operational mechanisms for reaching an outcome;
- **concept or direction exploration** — develop contrastive solution or creative territories;
- **counterhypothesis exploration** — articulate credible competing explanations or world models.

These are selectable regimes, not mandatory stages. Move between divergence, probing, reframing, and synthesis when feedback changes the space.

Use a narrower specialist method when domain validity depends on it.

## 3. Preserve truth and authority boundaries

Tag material inputs by their actual status:

- qualified fact or evidence;
- constraint;
- Human commitment or decision;
- Human preference or value;
- assumption;
- hypothesis;
- generated possibility.

Do not convert a generated possibility into a requirement, an assumption into evidence, or an observed reaction into a permanent Human preference.

Use available evidence directly. When acquiring, updating, reconciling, or qualifying missing evidence becomes a material primary task, route that task to `research-evidence` or a narrower research method.

## 4. Expand across meaningful difference

Generate alternatives that differ in mechanism, not merely surface treatment.

Useful differentiators include:

- causal mechanism;
- actor or owner;
- unit of value;
- sequence or timing;
- central assumption;
- resource model;
- degree of reversibility;
- scale or abstraction level;
- system boundary; and
- relationship between Human and AI work.

When credible, include:

- a simpler route;
- a no-action or delay route;
- a staged or reversible route;
- a route that changes the frame instead of solving within it; and
- a counterhypothesis that would make the apparent problem disappear or change owner.

Do not create a fixed quota of ideas. Generate enough meaningful contrast to test the frame.

## 5. Use contrastive probes for calibration

Use a probe when words alone are unlikely to reveal the distinction and the feedback could materially reshape the explored space.

A useful probe is:

- low resolution;
- cheap to discard;
- explicit about the dimension it contrasts;
- different enough to produce information; and
- not presented as a commitment or near-final product.

For Human taste, identity, values, or risk appetite:

1. show the smallest contrast set that can expose the distinction;
2. ask for relative reactions, not abstract preference declarations;
3. capture what specifically attracted or repelled the Human;
4. keep the inference local and provisional; and
5. revise the search space when the reaction reveals a new dimension.

Do not use Human review for facts or quality judgments the AI or an adequate professional method can resolve.

## 6. Synthesize without prematurely deciding

Cluster duplicates, preserve meaningful outliers, and make the space legible through distinguishing dimensions.

A useful synthesis may contain:

- alternative frames;
- a small set of mechanism-distinct directions;
- the core logic of each direction;
- assumptions and evidence status;
- tensions and trade-offs exposed by exploration;
- material unknowns;
- calibration signals; and
- the condition under which a direction becomes more or less promising.

Do not rank or recommend merely because a candidate set now exists. If a material bounded choice is ready, transfer it to `decision-analysis`.

## 7. Challenge premature collapse

Before closing, ask only the challenge questions capable of changing the space:

- Did all candidates inherit the same hidden frame?
- Are the differences cosmetic rather than causal?
- Is a simpler, no-action, delay, or staged route credibly missing?
- Did one salient example anchor the space?
- Is a constraint real, assumed, or preference-shaped?
- Does a credible counterhypothesis change the problem or its owner?
- Would a narrower professional method produce a materially different space?

Do not make adversarial challenge a ritual when it cannot alter the result.

## 8. Determine the next state

Use the smallest accurate next-state statement:

- `EXPLORATION_SUFFICIENT` — the parent episode has the required map or directions;
- `READY_FOR_FORMATION` — a now-visible missing basis must be bound before the next material transition;
- `READY_FOR_RESEARCH` — a material uncertainty requires evidence acquisition or qualification;
- `READY_FOR_DECISION` — the choice is sufficiently bounded for decision analysis;
- `ROUTE_TO_SPECIALIST` — a narrower professional exploration method should own the work;
- `NO_RESPONSIBLE_SPACE` — available basis cannot support a useful exploration without fabrication.

These states are routing signals, not acceptance, authorization, commitment, execution, or outcome claims.

## 9. Return the result

Return proportionately:

- bound purpose and open dimension;
- explored frames, routes, concepts, or hypotheses;
- distinguishing mechanisms and assumptions;
- evidence and uncertainty boundaries;
- calibration learned and its scope;
- what remains deliberately unresolved;
- stop rationale; and
- next state or responsible owner.

Use this as a semantic contract, not a mandatory visible template.

## 10. Stop rule

Stop when:

- the explored space is sufficient for its intended use;
- additional variants repeat existing mechanisms;
- feedback has stabilized the material dimensions;
- a different method or Human input is the true next need; or
- further exploration has lower expected value than transition.

Do not continue into research, decision, detailed production, evaluation, authorization, or execution without a separate task need and legitimate owner.

## Method basis

This method operationalizes CCR-01 in `foundation/CONCERNS-AND-REQUIREMENTS-v0.3.md`. Its adaptive divergence–convergence logic is consistent with the Design Council's Double Diamond account of iterative exploration and with Stanford d.school's emphasis on adaptive design abilities rather than one universal fixed process:

- https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/the-double-diamond/
- https://dschool.stanford.edu/stories/lets-stop-talking-about-the-design-process

Those sources inform the method basis; they do not establish runtime fitness of this Skill.
