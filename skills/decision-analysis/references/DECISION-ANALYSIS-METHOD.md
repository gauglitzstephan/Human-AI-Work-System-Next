# Decision Analysis Method v0.2

**Status:** cross-domain decision discipline; not a universal professional decision methodology.

## Contents

- Purpose and method basis
- Bind the decision
- Establish objectives, constraints and affected parties
- Form options and bind evidence
- Select the proportional decision regime and required operations
- Treat risk, uncertainty, formal tools and option value
- Assess additional information value
- Challenge and develop the recommendation
- Preserve authority and stop

## Purpose

Develop a transparent and robust recommendation for a bounded material choice while preserving evidence limits, Human values and decision authority.

A good decision analysis evaluates the choice using the information and preferences available at the decision point. It does not guarantee a good outcome.

## Method basis and limits

This method is a cross-domain synthesis informed by:

- INFORMS Decision Analysis practice for prescriptive choice under uncertainty and multiple objectives;
- NASA decision-analysis guidance for objectives, criteria, alternatives, uncertainty, robustness and value of information;
- HM Treasury Green Book and related UK multiple-criteria, uncertainty and futures guidance for objective-led option formation, business-as-usual, risk, sensitivity and switching analysis;
- the Society of Decision Professionals’ decision-quality elements as a secondary practitioner framework.

These sources qualify the cross-domain discipline, not every domain-specific input or decision.

Use formal probability, utility, MCDA or specialist operations only when their assumptions and professional application are adequate for the claim. Use a narrower qualified method for legal, medical, investment, safety-critical or other domain-governed decisions.

The synthesis is also informed by:

- naturalistic decision-making research on qualified expert judgment under genuine time pressure;
- Robust Decision Making and Dynamic Adaptive Policy Pathways for decisions under deep uncertainty;
- behavioral decision research on prospective failure reasoning and considering credible alternatives;
- Human–AI decision-support research on overreliance, verifiability and proportionate cognitive forcing.

Open-source sensitivity, simulation and robust-decision tools demonstrate available analytical operations. They are implementation options, not mandatory dependencies or independent qualification of the underlying model.

## 1. Bind the decision

Establish only as far as needed:

- the exact decision;
- the Decision Owner;
- the decision deadline or horizon;
- the current state and status quo;
- what is inside and outside the decision;
- the consequence of delay or non-decision;
- the required professional quality; and
- the available evidence.

Split materially different decision levels. Do not answer a portfolio, career or strategy question by choosing one operational action unless that is the bound decision.

## 2. Establish objectives and constraints

Separate:

- desired outcomes;
- hard constraints;
- material preferences;
- risk or loss tolerances;
- obligations and authority boundaries;
- proposed means; and
- evaluation criteria inherited from a qualified upstream method.

A hard constraint is non-compensatory. An option that violates it is not rescued by strength elsewhere.

Do not convert convenience, convention, an existing plan or a salient metric into an objective without justification.

## Materially affected parties and distribution

Distinguish the Decision Owner from parties materially affected by the alternatives.

Include another party only when an option could materially change their rights, obligations, welfare, safety, resources, exposure or ability to act. Do not create a generic stakeholder inventory.

Assess relevant distributional effects and externalities instead of relying only on an aggregate benefit or the Decision Owner’s private preference.

Do not invent values, trade-off weights or consent for affected parties. When their judgment or authority is non-substitutable, preserve it as an open input or condition.

## 3. Form a viable option set

Use the smallest option set capable of representing the real choice.

Consider where materially legitimate:

- the stated alternatives;
- status quo or business as usual;
- no action;
- delay or park;
- stop or kill;
- staged commitment;
- reversible experiment or probe;
- hybrid or conditional route; and
- an alternative at the correct higher or lower decision level.

Do not manufacture options merely to create symmetry. Do not rank alternatives while the primary need is still open exploration.

## 4. Bind evidence and assumptions

Use qualified evidence already available from native or specialist research, work-product evaluation, or other qualified methods. When a material premise can be investigated within the authorization, obtain that evidence through the available provider and incorporate it into the comparison. “Research required” is not a completed contribution when that research is accessible and part of the task.

Distinguish:

- observed facts;
- evaluated properties;
- assumptions;
- forecasts or scenarios;
- Human preferences;
- model outputs; and
- unresolved unknowns.

Do not treat an evaluation disposition as a decision. Do not treat a rationale, forecast, score or model as proof.

## Select the proportional decision regime

Use the shallowest regime capable of detecting the material decision failure modes.

Choose an operation with inputs adequate for its conclusion:

| Decision structure | Perform | Required basis | Supportable conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Binding constraint or dominance | Test feasibility first; compare remaining consequences | Actual constraint, comparable option properties | Exclusion or dominance on the specified basis |
| Consequential trade-off | Compare consequences in meaningful units; vary decisive assumptions/preferences | Evidence for consequences and legitimate priorities | Supported or conditional preference; explicit switching point |
| Characterizable risk | Expected-value/utility or probability analysis when useful | Defensible probabilities, consequence model and risk preferences | Model-conditional recommendation, with sensitivity |
| Genuine time pressure | Qualified expert judgment and rapid mental simulation | Relevant domain experience and recognizable situation | Time-bounded judgment; uncertainty exposed |
| Deep uncertainty | Test option vulnerabilities across plausible regimes; examine thresholds and adaptation | Credible mechanisms, relevant futures, consequence ranges where supportable | Scenario-conditional or robust preference on the examined basis |

These regimes are selectable, not mandatory stages. A qualitative matrix is a reasoning aid, not a calculated regret measure or evidence of robustness. Compare actual consequences where supported; do not invent scores, weights or probabilities to fill cells.

Do not force a matrix onto a clear decision. Do not treat generic pattern recognition as qualified expertise. Combine regimes only when the decision genuinely requires it.

## 5. Select only the required decision operations

### Constraint and dominance screening

Remove options that violate genuine hard constraints or are materially dominated.

### Consequence comparison

Compare material benefits, costs, burdens, risks, opportunity costs and downstream effects across the relevant time horizon.

### Scenario and robustness analysis

Test how options perform across relevant plausible futures, including the mechanisms that could defeat each option. A robustness claim needs demonstrated satisfactory performance across the examined conditions, or qualified dominance; naming scenarios and assigning qualitative ranks is insufficient. Expose a fragile ranking or return a conditional recommendation when support is weaker.

### Sensitivity and switching analysis

Identify which assumption, preference, threshold or forecast would need to change for another option to become preferable.

### Probability and expected-value or utility analysis

Use only when probabilities, consequences and risk preferences can be represented responsibly. Do not invent numerical precision.

### Multi-criteria analysis

Use only when multiple objectives require explicit comparison. Criteria must be non-duplicative enough for the intended use, and weights must represent legitimate preferences rather than arbitrary analyst choices. Test sensitivity to weights and scores.

### Reversibility and staged commitment

Assess whether learning can be purchased through a limited, reversible action without silently converting a probe into commitment.

These are selectable operations, not mandatory stages.

## Risk, model uncertainty and deep uncertainty

Distinguish:

- **risk**, where outcomes and probabilities are adequately characterizable;
- **model or parameter uncertainty**, where important assumptions or estimates remain unstable; and
- **deep uncertainty**, where relevant models, probabilities, future states or values cannot be specified responsibly or are materially contested.

Use expected value, probability or utility operations only when their inputs and assumptions are adequate.

Under deep uncertainty, compare robustness across plausible futures, material vulnerabilities, path dependencies, reversibility and the ability to adapt. Consider staged actions, signposts and explicit switching conditions.

Do not manufacture probabilities to create apparent precision.

An adaptive pathway is an option structure, not an implementation plan. Planning, monitoring implementation and execution remain with their responsible owners.

## Formal tools and calculations

Use transparent calculation, simulation, sensitivity analysis, decision trees, probability models or multi-criteria tools when:

- the decision structure supports them;
- the inputs and scales are sufficiently qualified; and
- the result could materially test or change the recommendation.

State material assumptions, operational definitions and sensitivity. Prefer deterministic checks where applicable.

Do not use a numerical score to override hard constraints, non-compensable values, material distributional effects or qualified professional judgment. Do not convert weak qualitative inputs into false precision.

Tool output is analytical evidence, not authority. Do not invoke a formal tool merely to create the appearance of rigor.

## 6. Examine asymmetric consequences and option value

Identify:

- unacceptable downside;
- irreversible loss;
- foreclosed alternatives;
- time-sensitive opportunities;
- lock-in and switching costs;
- dependencies;
- recovery paths; and
- the value of preserving future choice.

Do not recommend reversibility by default. Delay and optionality also have costs.

## 7. Assess additional information value

Further evidence or testing is decision-relevant only when it could credibly change:

- the viable option set;
- a hard-constraint judgment;
- the preferred option;
- a required safeguard; or
- the confidence appropriate for the consequence.

Specify the smallest question, observation or test that would reduce the material uncertainty.

Pursue additional information when its plausible decision value justifies its cost, delay, burden and risk of losing the option. Quantify that value only with a defensible basis. Perform authorized accessible inquiry now when it is part of the request; preserve a conditional result or exact blocker when it is not obtainable.

## 8. Challenge the emerging recommendation

Test material failure modes such as:

- the stated options hide a better staged or non-action route;
- one salient criterion dominates unrelated objectives;
- preferences are encoded as facts or analyst weights;
- sunk costs distort the choice;
- uncertainty is hidden behind a precise score;
- optimism, confirmation or status-quo bias drives the result;
- the recommendation depends on one fragile assumption;
- the downside exceeds the Decision Owner’s legitimate tolerance;
- delay destroys material option value;
- a reversible test is actually a commitment; or
- the analysis solves the wrong decision level.

Use premortems, counterfactuals or outside-view comparisons only where they can expose a material failure mode.

Apply the challenge to the actual comparison, rather than appending a generic risk warning:

1. identify the strongest credible rival at the same decision level;
2. state the strongest reason the leading option could fail;
3. determine the material assumption, threshold or event that would switch the recommendation; and
4. check whether the option remains preferable under a plausible adverse scenario.

If no meaningful rival remains because of a hard constraint or clear dominance, state that directly.

Use consider-the-opposite, prospective failure reasoning or another challenge operation only when it can materially improve the decision. Do not make a full premortem or adversarial ritual mandatory.

## 9. Develop the recommendation

Name:

- the recommended option;
- why it performs best for the bound objectives;
- which trade-offs are accepted;
- material conditions or safeguards;
- decisive evidence and assumptions;
- remaining uncertainty;
- robustness or sensitivity;
- falsifiers;
- revisit triggers; and
- the direct implication for the next transition.

When alternatives are too close or the ranking is fragile, return `NO_ROBUST_PREFERENCE`, a conditional recommendation or a reversible test rather than manufacturing certainty.

## 10. Preserve authority

Decision Analysis advises the Decision Owner.

It does not create:

- Human acceptance;
- organizational approval;
- commitment;
- budget or resource authorization;
- legal or governance authority;
- execution;
- successful transition; or
- outcome.

If the user has legitimately delegated a routine decision within an authorized scope, Native ChatGPT may act within that authority. This method itself does not create the delegation.

## 11. Stop rule

Stop when the bounded recommendation is adequately supported, a reversible or conditional route responsibly contains remaining uncertainty, or the missing basis prevents a stronger result.

The recommendation completes this transformation, not necessarily the task. Continue authorized evidence acquisition, product repair, strategy, planning, execution or monitoring through their appropriate native/domain methods and integrate the decision result. Preserve qualified state and reopen only dependencies affected by new evidence; do not manufacture another Human handoff.

## Worked examples — analyze what can decide the case

**Constraint dominance.** A purchase must work offline. A cheaper service requires an online connection; a more expensive device meets all binding requirements. Price cannot compensate for the failed offline constraint. Recommend the feasible device on this comparison if the purchase is otherwise justified; if its price violates a binding budget too, neither option is feasible. Do not invent weights to pick a winner.

**Sensitive trade-off.** Two feasible machines differ by 2,000 euros upfront and an evidenced 500 euros of annual operating cost. Ignoring discounting and other differences explicitly, the cost crossover is four years. A shorter intended life favors the cheaper purchase; a longer life favors lower operating cost. Test whether maintenance, uncertainty or a different horizon changes that conclusion. An unknown lifespan supports a conditional recommendation, not an invented preference.

**Deep uncertainty.** A proprietary service may become much cheaper, remain expensive or cease to meet access needs. Compare service-only, self-managed and export-capable hybrid routes on actual cost, switching burden, control and failure recovery under those regimes. If export cost or completeness is unknown, “hybrid is robust” is not yet established. Examine export/reconstruction evidence or recommend only a bounded commitment with a specific condition. Waiting also costs foregone use; it is not free optionality. Do not assign probabilities or select a future merely to settle the recommendation.
